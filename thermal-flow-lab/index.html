FROM YOUR STUDY TO A VOLUME

One matrix.
A three-dimensional network.

Every occupied cell is a small object from the original model. It stores heat, and exchanges it with up to six neighboring cells.

T(t + Δt) = Γ(Δt) T(t)
Γ = diag(1 − Mᵣ 1) + Mᵣ

The lab assembles the nonzero entries of Γ before iterating, then performs the same sparse matrix–vector multiplication at every full time step in a Web Worker. A fixed ambient temperature is appended to T as one extra node, so boundary convection is part of Γ too.

From shape to physics

Cubic cells of width Δx use m = ρΔx³ and R = 1/(kΔx). Boundary faces optionally exchange heat with fixed ambient air, with conductance hΔx².

Stable by construction

Δt ≤ 0.9 minᵢ(Cᵢ / ΣⱼGᵢⱼ). The stable Γ has nonnegative rows whose entries sum to one. Faster playback uses more multiplications, never an unsafe step.

What this model assumes

One homogeneous material, constant properties, conduction and optional linear convection. It excludes radiation, fluid flow, phase changes, mechanical motion and contact resistance. Touching occupied cells conduct; separated components do not. A held spot represents an external ideal thermostat.

The staircase volume grid approximates geometry. Thin walls and small gaps may disappear or connect at coarse resolution. Inspect “Volume cells” and compare resolutions. This is an exploratory simulator, not a validated engineering analysis.

Model dimensions are set explicitly in metres. Temperatures display in Celsius; differences in Celsius and kelvin are identical. Surface colors use nearby occupied cells; exported values are the actual volume-cell temperatures.
